My push / pull / legs split

I train with a push, pull, legs split. Each muscle group gets two different days: each day is built around one main lift, and the rest of the session is accessory work that supports it.

Push

Day 1 – main lift

  • Weighted dips

Day 2 – main lift

  • Handstand push-ups against the wall, with resistance band assistance

Accessories

  • Archer push-ups
  • Bench press
  • Straight bar dips
  • Triceps exercises

Pull

Day 1 – main lift

  • Weighted pull-ups

Day 2 – main lift

  • Muscle-ups

Accessories

  • Ice cream makers
  • Deadlifts
  • Lower back extensions
  • Rows
  • Inverted hanging rows

Legs

Day 1 – main lift

  • Pistol squats and weighted pistol squats

Day 2 – main lift

  • Heavy barbell squats

Accessories

  • Leg extensions
  • Hamstring curls
  • Bulgarian split squats
  • Calf raises
  • Hanging leg raises
  • L-sits

How a session goes

Every workout follows the same order, so the hardest work happens when I have the most energy.

  1. Warm-up (10 min) – light cardio, arm circles, band pull-aparts and a few easy sets of the main lift.
  2. Main lift – heavy, low reps (about 4–5 sets of 3–6 reps) with 2–3 minutes of rest between sets.
  3. Accessories – lighter, higher reps (about 3 sets of 8–12 reps) with 60–90 seconds of rest.
  4. Core finisher – L-sits or hanging leg raises.
  5. Cool-down – stretching for the muscles I just trained.

How I progress the main lifts

What I change once an exercise starts to feel easy
Main lift How I make it harder
Weighted dipsAdd 2.5 kg to the dip belt once I hit all my sets and reps.
Handstand push-upsSwitch to a lighter resistance band, until I can do them with no band at all.
Weighted pull-upsAdd 2.5 kg to the dip belt once I hit all my sets and reps.
Muscle-upsAdd more clean reps per set, with a slower and more controlled transition.
Pistol squatsGo from bodyweight to holding a dumbbell or kettlebell, then a heavier one.
Barbell squatsAdd 2.5–5 kg to the bar each week while keeping good depth and form.

Why it works

Higher-rep bodyweight sets and circuits also train your heart and lungs. Over time they can raise your VO2 max, which is a measure of how much oxygen your body can use during hard exercise.

Glossary

Rep
One complete repetition of an exercise, such as one push-up.
Set
A group of reps done back to back without resting.
AMRAP
“As many reps as possible” within a set or a time limit.
Main lift
The most important and hardest exercise of the day. I do it first, while I'm fresh.
Accessory
A lighter exercise done after the main lift to strengthen the muscles that support it.
Weighted
A bodyweight exercise done with extra weight, such as plates hanging from a dip belt.
Muscle-up
A pull-up that keeps going until you are above the bar, finishing with a dip on top of it.
Ice cream maker
A pull exercise where you hang from the bar and, with straight arms, raise your body until it is flat under the bar.
Handstand push-up
A push-up done upside down in a handstand, usually with your feet against a wall for balance.
Pistol squat
A single-leg squat all the way down while the other leg stays straight out in front.
L-sit
A hold where you support yourself on your hands with your legs straight out in front, making an L shape.
